## Hook
For two weeks, we looked at everything that can go wrong with ML — bad data, brittle models, failure modes that erode trust. Now we flip the lens. This week is about what happens when it works.

## Scene 1 — The Pivot
The same techniques we've been stress-testing — pattern recognition, predictive modelling, neural networks trained on large datasets — are running in production across healthcare, finance, manufacturing, and retail right now. Not in pilots. Not in proof-of-concept sandboxes. In live systems making real decisions.

## Scene 2 — The Engine Underneath
What makes this possible is the convergence of three things that have matured in parallel: the models themselves (ML, deep neural networks, NLP, computer vision), the datasets large enough to train them on, and the infrastructure to deploy them at scale. That last part is IT's domain — and it's why this week matters for this audience specifically.

## Scene 3 — What "Across Sectors" Actually Means
This isn't a vague claim. AI is driving demand forecasting in retail supply chains, automating authentication in banking, analysing video streams in logistics, and extracting text from images that used to require manual processing. Each of those is a system someone has to build, operate, and support. The business saw the outcome. IT owns the pipes.

## Takeaway
AI left the lab years ago — the question now is whether IT professionals are positioned to understand, support, and shape what it's doing in their organisation.
