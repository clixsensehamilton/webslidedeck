## Hook
Forty percent fewer account takeovers. Two and a half million dollars in avoided waste. Thirty percent more engagement. Those results didn't come from a data science team working in isolation — they came from systems that someone had to build, connect, and keep running.

## Scene 1 — IT as the Enabler
Every case study this week had an infrastructure layer underneath it: a pipeline delivering biometric data to an authentication model in real time, a data cleaning and aggregation workflow feeding a forecasting engine, a customer data platform that made segmentation inputs available to an ML model at scale. None of those outcomes were possible without someone owning the data flow, the deployment environment, and the operational reliability of the system. That's the IT function — whether or not it carries an AI label.

## Scene 2 — What Being Asked to Support Looks Like
The business case arrives before the architecture does. A stakeholder brings a problem — "we're losing accounts to takeover fraud," "our forecasts are consistently wrong," "we can't tell which customers to target" — and the expectation is that IT can evaluate what it takes to make an AI-based solution viable. That means understanding the data requirements, the integration points, the monitoring obligations, and the failure modes. This week gave you the vocabulary for that conversation.

## Scene 3 — One More Thing Before Next Week
This week showed AI working. Next week examines what's sitting in the shadow of these wins — bias built into the data before the model ever trains, decisions that can't be explained after the fact, privacy obligations that don't disappear because the system is automated. IT professionals who understand how these systems work are the ones best positioned to ask the right questions before deployment, not after.

## Takeaway
IT doesn't just support AI business cases — it shapes whether they're viable, defensible, and safe to operate at scale.

## Visual Direction
Understated design — no triumphalist iconography. Use a clean operational diagram showing an IT professional's view: data sources, pipeline, model, output, monitoring. Each component is labelled with the business outcome it enables (authentication model → 40% fewer takeovers; forecasting pipeline → $2.5M saved). The final scroll reveal adds a subtle visual shadow or question mark layer to the diagram — signalling that Week 12 will examine what sits underneath these wins. Tone should feel like a confident pause, not a celebration.
