## Hook
When business leaders talk about AI transforming operations, they're describing systems that sit inside infrastructure IT already manages. The transformation is real — and so is the ownership question.

## Scene 1 — Process Automation: AI in the Operational Layer
AI-driven automation has moved well beyond scripted workflows. Smart buildings use it to regulate energy consumption dynamically across sensors and systems. Energy grids use it to balance load in response to real-time demand signals. Automated contact centres route and resolve customer interactions without human escalation for the majority of cases. Supply chains use robotics guided by AI to manage warehouse movement, product picking, and inventory positioning. In each case, the AI is embedded in an operational process — and that process runs on infrastructure IT maintains.

## Scene 2 — Predictive Analytics: AI in the Decision Layer
Alongside automation sits a second category: predictive analytics models embedded directly into reporting dashboards. These aren't static reports — they run continuous inference against live data. Real-time event detection flags anomalies as they occur. Risk modelling scores transactions, customers, or operational states as conditions change. Customer profiling builds and updates segments on an ongoing basis. Business leaders use these dashboards to make decisions about revenue, operations, and risk — often without knowing there's a model running underneath. IT knows, because IT built the pipeline feeding it.

## Scene 3 — What Changes for IT
These two categories — automation and analytics — have different infrastructure signatures. Automation systems need reliable sensor feeds, low-latency response, and fault-tolerant processing at the edge. Analytics dashboards need clean, timely data pipelines, model refresh schedules, and monitoring for model drift. Both require IT to do something that wasn't in the job description five years ago: understand enough about what the AI component is doing to know whether it's still doing it correctly.

## Takeaway
AI in operations means two things for IT: automation that replaces human steps in live processes, and analytics models that replace human judgement in dashboards — both running on infrastructure IT owns.

## Visual Direction
Open with a schematic of a generic IT infrastructure diagram — servers, sensors, pipelines, dashboards. On the first scroll reveal, highlight the operational layer: smart building nodes, contact centre routing, warehouse robotics. On the second, highlight the analytics layer: data pipeline feeding a dashboard with live scoring indicators. Use subtle pulsing animations on the live data flows to signal real-time inference. Final frame labels both layers clearly within the same infrastructure diagram — positioning them as part of one system, not two separate initiatives.
