## Hook
Every system you manage is generating data. The question isn't whether AI will touch your infrastructure — it already has. The question is whether you understand what's running on it.

## Scene 1 — The Shift That's Already Happened
AI and machine learning aren't on the roadmap anymore — they're in production. The tools your organisation uses to filter email, route support tickets, flag anomalies, and personalise user experiences are already running models. IT didn't always choose them, and IT doesn't always know what they're doing under the hood.

## Scene 2 — Why "That's the Data Team's Problem" No Longer Holds
When an AI system fails, it fails in infrastructure. It chokes on a pipeline, hits a resource limit, produces outputs that nobody flagged as wrong until something broke downstream. The people closest to those systems — the ones who keep them running — are IT professionals. Understanding the mechanics isn't optional when you're responsible for uptime.

## Scene 3 — What This Module Actually Covers
This isn't a course about becoming a data scientist. It's about developing the operational literacy to work alongside AI systems, ask the right questions when something goes wrong, and evaluate what you're being asked to support. We'll move from fundamentals — what AI is, how it learns, where it fails — to real-world deployments and the ethical weight they carry.

## Scene 4 — The Right Question
The question isn't "should I care about AI?" You're already supporting it. The question is: do you know enough to manage it deliberately, or are you flying blind on systems that make real decisions? This series is the answer to that question.

## Takeaway
You're not on the outside of the AI conversation — you're in the middle of it, managing the systems it runs on.
