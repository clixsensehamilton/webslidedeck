## Hook
When someone says "data science," most IT professionals picture a role — a person with a Python notebook and a PhD. That's not wrong. But it misses something more useful: data science is a structure, and you already work inside part of it.

## Scene 1 — Three Disciplines, One Stack
Data science sits at the intersection of three distinct knowledge domains. The first is domain knowledge — understanding the business problem well enough to know what a useful answer looks like. The second is computer science — the engineering muscle that moves, stores, transforms, and serves data. The third is mathematics and statistics — the algorithms and numerical methods that extract signal from noise.

## Scene 2 — What Happens When You Remove One
These three pillars don't just combine — they produce distinct things depending on which one is missing. Business knowledge plus computer science, without the math, produces traditional software: applications that execute rules, not learn from data. Business knowledge plus math, without the engineering, produces traditional research: rigorous analysis that rarely ships. Computer science plus math, without domain knowledge, produces machine learning that can't be trusted — technically correct outputs with no meaningful grounding in the problem being solved.

## Scene 3 — Where All Three Meet
When domain expertise, engineering capability, and mathematical rigour align in a single workflow, the result is data science: the ability to turn raw data into information, information into insight, and insight into a decision someone can act on. That's the actual definition. Not a job title. A capability stack.

## Scene 4 — Where You Already Sit
The computer science pillar isn't abstract — it's data engineering, data wrangling, pipeline construction, systems infrastructure, and yes, sometimes ethical hacking and access governance. That's IT territory. Before anyone trains a model or runs an algorithm, the data has to exist somewhere, be in the right format, and be accessible to the right systems. That's not a data scientist's job. That's yours.

## Takeaway
Data science is a three-pillar stack — and IT owns the pillar that everything else depends on.
