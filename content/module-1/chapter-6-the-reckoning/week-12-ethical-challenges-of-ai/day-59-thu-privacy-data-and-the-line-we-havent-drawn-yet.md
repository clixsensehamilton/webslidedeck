## Hook
The more capable the AI system, the more data it needs. The more data it holds, the higher the cost of getting privacy wrong.

## Scene 1 — Privacy as a Critical Ethical Issue in AI
Privacy and data security are explicitly identified in Video 12 as critical ethical concerns as AI becomes more deeply embedded in business operations. This isn't about abstract principles — it's about what AI systems actually do with sensitive personal data: they ingest it, pattern-match against it, and use it to make or influence decisions about individual people. The risk isn't just data breach. It's the misuse of data that was collected legitimately for one purpose and is now being leveraged for another.

## Scene 2 — The Scale Problem
Rule-based systems had limited surface area. They processed defined inputs and produced defined outputs. AI systems, particularly those trained on rich datasets, operate at a scale of inference that changes the privacy calculus. A model trained on customer demographics, financial behaviour, and location history doesn't just store that data — it uses the relationships between data points to generate predictions about individuals. Those predictions carry privacy implications even when the underlying data was appropriately collected.

## Scene 3 — The Obligation to Protect
Safeguarding user privacy and ensuring data security are described in the source material as essential for responsible AI use. That obligation doesn't sit with the model alone — it sits with every layer of the system that handles data, from ingestion to storage to output. For IT professionals, that means understanding what data an AI system processes, how long it's retained, who has access to inferences generated from it, and whether the people whose data is involved have been informed and have consented to its use in this context.

## Takeaway
The line between what AI is technically capable of doing with data and what it should be permitted to do is not drawn by the model — it's drawn by the people who design, deploy, and govern the system.

## Visual Direction
Use a boundary or threshold metaphor. Show a data flow — personal attributes and behavioural signals feeding into an AI system — with a visible line representing the privacy boundary. Scene 1 establishes the flow. Scene 2 shows the boundary being approached as scale increases. Scene 3 labels the obligation markers along the boundary: collection, retention, inference, access, consent. The line itself should remain visible throughout — the point is that it exists, that it matters, and that it requires active maintenance rather than passive assumption.
