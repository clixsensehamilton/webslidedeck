## Hook
Every ethical failure mode covered this week — bias, opacity, discrimination, privacy exposure — lives inside infrastructure that IT builds and operates. That's not a moral argument. It's an operational one.

## Scene 1 — IT as the Last Line of Defence
The ethical risks of AI don't exist at the level of policy documents and principles. They exist at the level of data pipelines, model deployment configurations, access controls, and audit logs. IT professionals are the last functional layer between an AI system's design intent and its real-world impact on users. When bias propagates, when a privacy boundary is crossed, or when an audit surfaces an unexplainable decision, the trail leads back to systems that IT owns.

## Scene 2 — Technical and Organisational Responsibility Together
"Building systems you can defend" means two things simultaneously. Technically: the system produces outputs that can be traced, audited, and explained. Organisationally: there is a clear chain of accountability for who reviewed the training data, who certified the model, who monitors for drift, and who gets called when something produces a harmful outcome. Neither is sufficient without the other. A technically transparent system with no governance process isn't defensible. A governance process built around a black-box system isn't either.

## Scene 3 — What This Looks Like in Practice
Fairness and equal treatment should be prioritised in AI development and deployment — that's the sourced principle. In practice, for IT, it translates to specific questions before any AI system goes live: Was the training data reviewed for representational gaps? Can the model's outputs be audited against protected categories? Is there a documented process for handling challenged decisions? Who owns the remediation path if the model produces discriminatory outcomes at scale? These aren't gatekeeping questions. They're the same diligence IT applies to any production system with real consequences.

## Takeaway
The organisations that build AI responsibly won't just be doing the right thing — they'll be the ones who can still operate when the audit lands.
