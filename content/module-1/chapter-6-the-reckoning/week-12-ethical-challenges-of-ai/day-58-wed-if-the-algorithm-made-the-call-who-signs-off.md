## Hook
An AI system flags a procurement vendor as high risk. A candidate doesn't advance. A compliance exception gets denied. The model made the call — but who owns it?

## Scene 1 — The Transparency Problem Is a Governance Problem
AI decisions are not always intelligible to the humans responsible for them. That's not a UX complaint — it's a regulatory and organisational liability. In environments with audit requirements, HR obligations, or procurement compliance rules, every significant decision needs to be explainable, defensible, and attributable to a responsible party. When an AI system produces a recommendation and no one can articulate how it arrived there, accountability becomes a gap in the process.

## Scene 2 — What "Lack of Transparency" Actually Means Operationally
Transparency in AI doesn't mean publishing the model's weights. It means being able to answer, in plain terms: what inputs drove this output, what factors were weighted most heavily, and would a different input have produced a different result? Without that, a challenge to an AI-assisted decision — from an employee, a vendor, a regulator, or an auditor — cannot be answered with the rigour the challenge deserves. Ensuring transparency is described in the source material as essential for accountability and trust.

## Scene 3 — The IT Professional's Position in This Chain
IT professionals in regulated environments are increasingly in the certification and audit path for AI-assisted decisions. That means understanding not just whether a system is running correctly, but whether its outputs can be interrogated. Can you produce a log of what the model evaluated? Can you demonstrate that the decision criteria are consistent and non-discriminatory? These are not hypothetical questions — they are the questions compliance and legal teams will bring to IT when something gets challenged.

## Takeaway
Transparency in AI is not about making the model simpler — it's about making accountability traceable, and that traceability is a governance responsibility that lands on the people who build and maintain the system.
