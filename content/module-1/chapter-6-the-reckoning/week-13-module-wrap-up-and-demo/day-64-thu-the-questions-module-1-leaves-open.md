## Hook
A solid foundation doesn't answer everything. It tells you which questions are now worth asking properly.

## Scene 1 — What This Module Was Designed to Do
Module 1 scoped itself deliberately. It built a foundational understanding of AI and ML — the concepts, the mechanics, the failure modes, and the ethical implications. That scope was enough to move from passive familiarity to functional fluency. What it wasn't designed to do is resolve every open question in a field that researchers, engineers, and regulators are still actively working through. Leaving certain questions open isn't a gap in the material — it's an accurate representation of where the field stands.

## Scene 2 — The Questions That Remain
How do you reliably detect and correct bias in a model that has already been deployed? How do you audit the decision-making of a complex model in a way that satisfies a regulator who didn't train ML? Where exactly does the accountability boundary sit between the data team, the model team, and the IT operations team when an AI-assisted decision causes harm? How do you maintain fairness guarantees when a model is retrained on new data? These aren't rhetorical. They are active areas of technical and policy development. Module 1 equipped you to understand why they are hard — not to pretend they are solved.

## Scene 3 — Why Intellectual Honesty Matters Here
The alternative — presenting AI as a solved problem with a clear playbook — would be more comfortable and less useful. IT professionals who encounter real AI systems in production will face precisely these unresolved tensions. The value of knowing a question is open, rather than falsely closed, is that you approach it with the right posture: not looking for a configuration setting that fixes bias, not assuming the audit trail exists, not delegating accountability without checking where it actually lands. Knowing the edges of your knowledge is itself a professional competence.

## Takeaway
Module 1 closes with open questions not because it ran out of answers — but because those questions require more than a foundational module to resolve, and pretending otherwise would be the least useful thing this series could do.

## Visual Direction
Visualise the open questions as unlabelled doors at the edge of a map — the map being a clean overhead view of the six chapters covered. The areas within the map are detailed and labelled. Beyond the boundary, the terrain becomes schematic and unfinished — not blank, but clearly in-progress. Each scene adds one unlabelled door to the edge. This should feel like intellectual curiosity, not incompleteness — the visual tone is expansive, not ominous. The doors are open, not locked.
