## Hook
You don't have to build an AI system to hold one accountable. You do have to know what questions to ask.

## Scene 1 — The Questions You Can Now Ask
Module 1 built a specific set of interrogation capabilities. About any AI system you encounter, evaluate, or are asked to support, you can now ask — and understand the answers to — the following: What type of learning does this system use, and what does that mean for how it handles new data? What was it trained on, and who is and isn't represented in that training set? Is it a classification system or a regression system, and what is it actually optimising for? How was the model's performance measured, and against what baseline? What failure modes — overfitting, underfitting, data drift — could affect its accuracy in production? What ethical risks does it carry, and where does accountability sit when something goes wrong?

## Scene 2 — What Each Question Unlocks
These aren't decorative questions. Each one probes a specific system property. The learning type question establishes whether the model can adapt to new inputs or whether it requires a full retraining cycle — which has infrastructure and change management implications. The training data question surfaces representational risk before deployment. The optimisation question reveals whether the system is aligned with the business outcome it's supposed to serve. The evaluation question distinguishes a genuinely performant model from one that merely beat a weak baseline. The failure modes question is the production risk assessment. The ethics and accountability question is the governance audit. Together, they constitute a framework for engaging with any AI system as a professional, not a bystander.

## Scene 3 — What This Means Going Forward
You are now equipped with a foundational understanding of the key concepts, applications, and challenges of AI and ML — that's the module's stated outcome, sourced from Video 13. In operational terms: you can participate in procurement evaluations of AI systems with informed questions. You can challenge claims about model performance using the right vocabulary. You can identify where in the AI lifecycle your organisation's data and infrastructure create risk. You can have a conversation with a data science team without needing a translator. That's the practical output of Module 1 — not expertise in building models, but fluency in interrogating the ones that will be built around you.

## Takeaway
The professionals who will shape how AI is used in their organisations aren't the ones who waited to understand it — they're the ones who started asking the right questions.
