## Hook
AGI is not a product. It's a research objective — one that's been the horizon of AI development for decades and remains unsolved. Understanding what it actually requires explains why.

## Scene 1 — What Makes AGI Different from ANI
The defining property of Artificial General Intelligence is domain transfer. An AGI system would be able to understand a problem in one area and apply reasoning developed in a completely different area to solve it. It doesn't need separate training for each task — it accumulates general understanding the way humans do and applies it fluidly across contexts. That's the fundamental gap between what's been built and what AGI would represent.

## Scene 2 — Where the Research Is Now
Fully functional AGI has not been achieved. What researchers are working toward includes systems that can adapt to unstructured, unpredictable environments — the kind of tasks that require improvisation rather than pattern matching. An example in active development: autonomous robots designed to navigate unstructured settings like homes or small businesses, where the environment is variable and no single pre-programmed response covers every situation. The robot needs to figure things out, not just execute a fixed sequence.

## Scene 3 — The Distance Between ANI and AGI
The gap is not primarily a compute problem. More processing power running the same ANI architecture doesn't produce AGI. The challenge is architectural — how does a system build generalised representations of knowledge that transfer across domains? Current deep learning approaches excel within their training distribution but struggle to generalise outside it in the way AGI would require. This is an active and unsolved area of AI research, not a roadmap item with a delivery date.

## Takeaway
AGI is the capability to reason across any domain — not a bigger ANI, but a fundamentally different kind of system that hasn't been built yet.
