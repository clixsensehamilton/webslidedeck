## Hook
After a week of definitions, here's the map: ANI is deployed and operational. AGI is being actively researched. ASI is theoretical. Most AI hype lives in the gap between these three facts.

## Scene 1 — The Honest State of the Field
Everything your organisation currently uses, evaluates, or procures under the label "AI" is ANI. That's not a criticism — ANI systems are genuinely powerful and increasingly capable. But the gap between ANI and AGI is a fundamental unsolved problem, not a product generation away. Any briefing that describes current AI as approaching general intelligence is describing something that doesn't exist yet.

## Scene 2 — Why This Map Is an IT Professional's Tool
When vendors pitch AI capabilities, the ANI/AGI/ASI framework gives you a precise question to ask: what is the specific task this system is trained to perform? If the answer is well-scoped and singular, you're dealing with ANI — evaluate it on its domain performance. If the answer is vague or implies broad general reasoning, ask for specifics. The spectrum isn't academic; it's a procurement and evaluation filter.

## Scene 3 — What the Trajectory Means for Planning
The direction of travel is clear: AI systems are becoming more capable, more embedded in enterprise infrastructure, and more consequential in their decisions. IT professionals who understand where the technology actually is — rather than where the hype says it is — are better positioned to design infrastructure that scales appropriately, establish governance before it's urgently needed, and evaluate claims without being misled by the terminology.

## Takeaway
ANI is real and running. AGI is research. ASI is theoretical. Calibrating your expectations to that map is one of the most practical things you can do as an IT professional right now.
