## Hook
"AI" gets used as if it describes one thing. It doesn't. It describes a spectrum — and most of the industry is clustered at one end of it.

## Scene 1 — Three Stages, Not Three Different Technologies
Artificial intelligence isn't a single capability. Researchers and practitioners use three categories to describe how capable an AI system is relative to human intelligence: Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI), Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), and Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI). These aren't product tiers. They're developmental stages — each one representing a fundamentally different level of cognitive scope.

## Scene 2 — What Separates Each Stage
The dividing line isn't processing speed or model size. It's scope. ANI systems are experts at one task and useless at everything else. An AGI system could, in principle, reason across entirely different domains — switching from medical diagnosis to financial analysis to creative writing without needing separate specialised training. ASI would surpass the full range of human cognitive ability across every domain simultaneously. Each stage is a qualitative leap, not a quantitative one.

## Scene 3 — Why the Distinction Matters Right Now
When a vendor says "AI-powered," they mean ANI — every time. The gap between ANI and AGI is not a software update away; it's an unsolved research problem. Understanding the spectrum keeps you grounded when evaluating claims, reading product roadmaps, or sitting in briefings where "AI" and "general intelligence" are used interchangeably. This week maps each stage in detail so you know exactly what you're looking at.

## Takeaway
AI is a spectrum with three defined stages — and the honest answer to "where are we?" is: firmly at stage one, with stage two still being actively researched.
