## Hook
The AI your organisation is already running isn't hypothetical or experimental. It's ANI — Artificial Narrow Intelligence — and it's embedded in tools your team manages every day.

## Scene 1 — What ANI Actually Is
Artificial Narrow Intelligence is defined by specialisation. An ANI system is trained to do one thing — and it does that one thing at a level that matches or exceeds human performance. The key constraint: it cannot transfer what it knows to an adjacent task. A spam filter that classifies email with near-perfect accuracy cannot tell you whether a network packet is malicious. It wasn't built to. Its intelligence is a mile deep and an inch wide.

## Scene 2 — Where ANI Lives in IT Environments
Look at the tools your team already operates. The chatbot on your service desk portal — ANI. The voice assistant routing calls through your contact centre — ANI. The spam and phishing filter protecting your mail gateway — ANI. The recommendation engine surfacing content on internal knowledge platforms — ANI. The facial recognition system at building access points — ANI. Every one of these is a specialised model doing exactly one job, exactly as designed.

## Scene 3 — What ANI's Limits Mean in Practice
Because ANI systems are single-purpose, deploying more AI capability means deploying more discrete ANI systems — each with its own training data requirements, performance envelope, and maintenance needs. There is no general-purpose AI you can point at a new problem. Each new capability is a new system. That's not a flaw; it's the current state of the technology. Understanding this shapes how you evaluate procurement proposals and architecture decisions that involve AI components.

## Takeaway
Every AI system your organisation uses today is ANI — deeply capable within its domain, completely blind outside it.
