### Video 3: Types of AI
Artificial intelligence isn't just the single technology, but spectrum of capabilities and 
potentials. We'll explore three distinct types of AI: one, Artificial Narrow Intelligence 
or  ANI,  two,  Artificial  General  Intelligence  or  AGI,  and  three,  Artificial  Super 
Intelligence, which is ASI. Each represents a different stage in AI development, each 
with its unique impact and potential applications. First, let's discuss Artificial Narrow 
Intelligence or ANI. This is the type of AI that surrounds us today. ANI specialised in 
tasks  specific.  Think  of  voice  assistants,  customer  service,  chat  bots,  and  even 
recommendation  systems  on  streaming  services.  Each  is  designed  to  perform  a 
particular task and does it extremely well. A real world example is the use of ANI in 
healthcare  for  diagnostic  assistance.  AI  systems  analyse  medical  images  to  detect

diseases such as cancer more quickly and accurately than ever before. This not only 
speeds up diagnosis, but also increases the precision, enhancing patient outcomes. 
Next, we have Artificial General Intelligence or AGI. Unlike ANI, AGI can understand, 
learn,  and  apply  knowledge  across  multiple  fields.  It's  what  we  often  see  in  sci-fi 
fields. An AI that can think like a human, solve general problems, and perform any 
intellectual tasks that a human being can. While fully functional AGI has not yet been 
achieved, advancements are being made. 

An  example  in  progress  is  autonomous  robots  being  developed  for  unstructured 
environments  like  homes  or  small  businesses,  where  they  need  to  adapt  to  a  wide 
range of tasks and scenarios. Lastly, we come to Artificial Super Intelligence, or ASI. 
ASI represents an AI that surpasses human intelligence and capability. It's an AI that 
can outperform the best human brains in practically even field, including scientific 
creativity, general wisdom, and social skills. ASI remains a theoretical concept, but 
one  that  drives  discussions  about  the  future  of  AI  technology  and  its  impact  on 
society.  The  potential  realisation  of  ASI  prompts  important  ethical  and  safety 
discussions,  ensuring  that  as  we  advance  in  AI  capabilities,  we  also  safeguard  our 
societal values.

Types of Machine Learning

Supervised Learning

Semi-Supervised Learning

•

Supervised learning involves training a model on labelled 
data, where each data point is paired with a corresponding 
label. The goal is to learn a mapping between inputs 
(features) and outputs (labels)

• Used for tasks like classification (categorising data into 

predefined groups) and regression (predicting continuous 
numerical values)

• Combines a small amount of labelled data with a large 

amount of unlabelled data to improve learning 
performance

•

Ideal for scenarios where obtaining labelled data is 
expensive or time-consuming but a large volume of 
unlabelled data is available

Unsupervised Learning

Reinforcement Learning (RL)

•

unsupervised learning works on unlabelled data to 
discover hidden patterns or structures within the dataset

• Used for tasks like clustering (grouping similar data points 
together) and dimensionality reduction (simplifying data 
while retaining important information)

• Reinforcement learning, an agent learns by interacting 

with an environment and receiving feedback in the form of 
rewards or penalties for its actions

• RL focuses on optimizing long-term outcomes through trial 
and error, making it suitable for dynamic and complex 
decision-making tasks
